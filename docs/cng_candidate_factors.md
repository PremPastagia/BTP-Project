# CNG forecasting: candidate factor reference

Saved: 2026-11-04

Decision: retain all 21 factor groups below for model planning and later testing. This is the full candidate inventory, not a decision to include every variable simultaneously. Selection, data availability, historical availability, and incremental predictive value remain to be assessed. News-derived variables remain candidates alongside numerical inputs.

| Factor group | Factors to consider | Why they could help |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past CNG prices | Previous prices, recent changes, time since last revision | Capture price persistence and revision patterns |
| Domestic gas prices | APM price, applicable ceilings, new-well gas prices | Determine part of distributors’ procurement costs |
| Gas sourcing mix | Domestic allocation, domestic/imported shares, spot/contract shares | The same benchmark increase affects distributors differently depending on sourcing |
| Imported LNG costs | Actual landed LNG cost, Asian LNG benchmarks, contract prices | Measure the cost of imported supply |
| Exchange rate | INR/USD and recent changes | Dollar-denominated gas becomes more expensive when the rupee weakens |
| Crude oil prices | Indian crude basket, Brent | Relevant through domestic pricing rules and some LNG contracts |
| Transport costs | Pipeline tariffs, tariff zones, regasification charges, trucking distance | Explain delivered-cost changes and geographical differences |
| Station operating costs | Electricity tariffs, compression energy, wages, maintenance, station utilisation | Affect the cost of converting delivered gas into retail CNG |
| Taxes and charges | Applicable state taxes, duties, local charges, tax-policy changes | Directly affect final retail prices |
| Distributor behaviour | Margins, procurement contracts, pricing strategy, cost-pass-through delays | Costs may be absorbed temporarily or passed on gradually |
| Local market structure | City/distributor identity, competition, network maturity, pipeline connectivity | Explain persistent differences between locations |
| Domestic supply | Gas production, field outages, maintenance, allocation shortages | Shortfalls may require more expensive replacement gas |
| Global gas supply | LNG production, export outages, shipping costs, terminal capacity | Affect imported gas availability and cost |
| Competing global demand | Asian/European consumption, storage, heating/cooling demand | Can raise competition for LNG cargoes |
| Transport demand | CNG vehicle fleet, registrations, public transport use, freight activity, mobility | Indicate local fuel demand and capacity pressure |
| Alternative fuels | Petrol/diesel prices, relative running costs, EV adoption | Influence CNG adoption and demand |
| Economic activity | Industrial production, GDP, consumption/income, inflation | Potential indirect demand and operating-cost indicators |
| Policy changes | Gas allocation rules, pricing formulas, transport mandates, environmental rules | Can change costs or demand abruptly |
| Disruptions and news | Wars, sanctions, strikes, accidents, shipping restrictions, cyclones | May signal emerging supply or cost pressures |
| Calendar and weather | Month, holidays, monsoon, extreme temperatures | Possible demand, logistics, and global LNG effects; local relevance needs testing |
| Expectations | Available futures prices, announced tariff changes, scheduled maintenance | Provide forward-looking information available before the forecast |

Source: user-confirmed factor inventory in the BTP planning chat, 2026-11-04. Rationales are candidate mechanisms, not established causal findings for this dataset.
