# BTP — FINAL RESEARCH PROTOCOL: STAGES 4–9
## Critical, Evidence-Driven and Reproducible Research

**Repository:** https://github.com/PremPastagia/BTP-Project
**Current stage:** Stage 3 — Data Collection and Verification

### Your role

Act as an independent research statistician, econometrician, machine-learning engineer, data-quality auditor and scientific reviewer.

Read the finalized Stage 1–3 documentation and repository before proceeding. Preserve the approved research design without repeating or unnecessarily changing it.

Your responsibility is **to discover what the evidence supports, not to prove our hypotheses correct**.

## 1. Non-negotiable scientific rules

1. Never fabricate observations, references, statistical results or model performance.
2. Treat GAM superiority, H1 numerical predictors and H2 textual information as hypotheses—not established facts.
3. Accept negative, contradictory and inconclusive findings.
4. Never alter data, exclusions, metrics or test periods simply to obtain a favorable result.
5. Preserve original datasets, code, failed experiments and previous results.
6. Correct errors only using independent evidence and documented methodology.
7. Distinguish observed facts, derived quantities, estimated proxies and unsupported assumptions.
8. Prevent future-information leakage at every forecasting origin.
9. Do not confuse predictive relationships with causal effects.
10. Require independent verification of critical calculations and findings.
11. Account for statistical power, multiple testing, uncertainty and model-selection bias.
12. Maintain a complete, auditable experiment registry.
13. Stop whenever a critical validity requirement is unsatisfied.
14. Never claim absolute accuracy or certainty without sufficient evidence.

**A sophisticated model with unreliable evidence is less valuable than a simple model with trustworthy results.**

# 3. Stage 4 — Preprocessing, Integration and EDA

**Objective:** Construct trustworthy analytical datasets and understand their observed properties.

Tasks:

- Verify data types, keys, geographic identifiers, units, temporal grain, duplicates and missingness.
- Independently reconstruct and check month-end CNG prices and time-weighted monthly averages.
- Preserve original data frequency, reporting periods, effective dates, publication dates and extraction dates.
- Avoid backward-filling, invented observations and undocumented interpolation.
- Make all transformations reproducible and reversible where possible.
- Handle numerical variables, regional factors, event records and textual sources separately before justified integration.
- Investigate trends, seasonality, price persistence, revision frequency, correlations, distribution differences and outliers.
- Compare cities and CGD operators without assuming their pricing structures are identical.
- Document whether each observed relationship is descriptive, potentially predictive or unsupported.

**Additional safeguard — Independent verification**

Recalculate important quantities using an independent implementation or independently specified calculation.

For example, verify TWAP calculations with a separate algorithm and hand-checked revision examples rather than relying only on the original aggregation function.

Reconciliation failures must be investigated and documented.

**Deliverables:** Versioned analytical datasets, data dictionary, EDA report, transformation logs, independently checked target calculations and unresolved-issues register.

**Gate:** No Stage 5 until target definitions and all consequential transformations are verified.

---

# 4. Stage 5 — Stationarity, Structural Breaks and Statistical Properties

**Objective:** Determine which time-series assumptions are actually defensible.

Investigate:

- Persistence, trends and seasonality.
- Autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation.
- ADF, KPSS and other justified stationarity tests.
- Variance instability and structural breaks.
- Policy changes, COVID-era events, LNG shocks and allocation disruptions.
- Price-level versus differenced/log-transformed specifications.
- Differences among cities, periods and forecast targets.

Do not force stationarity or assume every detected break is caused by an identified policy.

Explain test null hypotheses, sample-size limitations, statistical power and consequences for model identification.

**Additional safeguard — Statistical power**

Evaluate whether there are enough independent observations, price changes, forecast origins and market regimes to support meaningful inference.

Account for autocorrelation and overlapping multi-horizon forecasts, which reduce effective independent information.

If the sample is insufficient for a proposed model or test, report the limitation and choose a simpler method or mark the question inconclusive.

**Deliverables:** Statistical diagnostics, structural-break assessment, justified transformation recommendations, power/feasibility assessment and limitations.

**Gate:** Advance only when the proposed modeling assumptions are supported or explicitly qualified.

# 5. Stage 6 — Model Identification and Experimental Design

**Objective:** Establish a defensible comparison protocol before inspecting final test results.

### Mandatory baselines

Include appropriate simple forecasting methods:

- Last-observed-price/persistence forecast.
- Naïve and drift benchmarks where justified.
- Seasonal naïve when the data supports seasonality.
- Suitable elementary statistical models.

### Candidate models

Evaluate suitability before implementation:

- ARIMA/SARIMA and exogenous extensions.
- ETS where appropriate.
- GAM with penalized smooths and defensible flexibility.
- Regularized regression.
- Suitable tree-based boosting methods.
- Individual-city models.
- Pooled/panel or hierarchical multi-city approaches.

Do not run every algorithm mechanically.

### Experimental requirements

- Preserve both forecasting targets and 1-, 3- and 6-month horizons.
- Define forecast origins, training windows, validation windows and an untouched final test.
- Use chronological walk-forward or expanding-window evaluation.
- Define the treatment of exogenous predictors that are unknown at the forecast origin.
- Predefine primary metrics, model families, tuning procedures and comparison criteria.
- Compare models on matched cities, dates and observation sets whenever feasible.
- Use sufficiently conservative complexity relative to the available effective sample.

### H1/H2 ablations

Where verified datasets permit, plan:

A. Historical-price-only baseline.

B. Historical prices plus H1 numerical information.

C. H1 plus independent news information.

D. H1 plus corporate disclosures.

E. H1 plus both textual sources.

F. Bias-aware extensions, only if supported by sufficient data.

No text method should be included merely to satisfy the original hypothesis.

**Additional safeguard — Multiple testing**

Prevent false discoveries from extensive searches across variables, transformations, hyperparameters, cities, models, feature groups and horizons.

Maintain a predeclared set of primary comparisons.

Use appropriate multiplicity adjustments or clearly labeled exploratory findings for families of statistical tests.

Do not repeatedly change the hypothesis after inspecting results and then present the final version as confirmatory.

**Deliverables:** Approved experimental protocol, comparison matrix, baselines, tuning rules, power considerations and primary/secondary hypothesis definitions.

**Gate:** Freeze the main experimental design before final evaluation.

# 6. Stage 7 — Model Estimation and Diagnostics

**Objective:** Fit models correctly and determine whether their behavior is statistically and economically credible.

Tasks:

- Fit preprocessing transformations and hyperparameters using training data only.
- Use only information available at the applicable forecast origin.
- Maintain reproducible training configurations and software environments.
- Examine residual dependence, heteroskedasticity, parameter stability and misspecification where appropriate.
- For GAM, examine smoothing complexity, effective degrees of freedom, concurvity, basis adequacy and extrapolation.
- For ML, examine overfitting, instability, generalization and sensitivity to predictors.
- Distinguish statistically supported findings from interpretive speculation.
- Investigate implausible results rather than automatically accepting favorable accuracy.

**Additional safeguard — Independent result verification**

Reproduce important model outputs using fresh runs and independently checked evaluation calculations.

For selected critical results, verify predictions, target alignment, metric arithmetic and feature-availability dates through an alternative implementation or independently written test.

Identical results from two scripts sharing the same faulty helper do not constitute independent verification.

**Failure protocol:** Preserve the failed model, diagnose the cause and test only justified alternatives. Never tune repeatedly against the final holdout until results appear favorable.

**Deliverables:** Model artifacts, training logs, diagnostic reports, independent verification checks and experiment registry.

**Gate:** Only sufficiently validated results proceed to final comparison.

# 7. Stage 8 — Forecasting and Hypothesis Testing

**Objective:** Evaluate whether the models genuinely improve out-of-sample forecasting.

### Forecasting evaluation

Report:

- MAE and RMSE in ₹/kg.
- Performance by city.
- Performance by target.
- Performance at each forecast horizon.
- Performance across appropriate historical evaluation periods.
- Explicitly defined aggregated metrics.
- Forecast bias and uncertainty measures when supported.

Compare every relevant complex model against simple baselines.

Use identical forecast origins and matched evaluation samples wherever feasible.

### Critical H1 evaluation

Test whether verified numerical factors actually improve forecasts beyond historical-price information.

Possible outcomes:

- Supported.
- Partially/conditionally supported.
- Not supported.
- Inconclusive.

Do not force H1 to succeed.

### Critical H2 evaluation

Assess whether genuinely dated news, corporate reports or bias-aware features add incremental predictive information beyond the numerical baseline.

Investigate duplication, publication timing, source disagreement, instability, overfitting and sample-size limitations.

If additional text features fail, document the negative result.

### Additional safeguard — Uncertainty quantification

Where methodologically justified, provide:

- Prediction intervals and empirical interval coverage.
- Confidence intervals for forecasting error differences.
- Dependence-aware bootstrap or other appropriate interval methods.
- Uncertainty in estimated model effects.
- Sensitivity of conclusions to sample period and model specification.

Account for serial dependence, common shocks across cities and overlapping forecast horizons.

Do not present narrow intervals as evidence of certainty without evaluating their assumptions and calibration.

### Additional safeguard — Multiplicity and robust comparison

When many model comparisons are tested, address multiple-comparison risk.

Use formal forecast-comparison tests only when their assumptions are appropriate.

Avoid declaring a model superior merely because its RMSE is numerically smaller on one test period.

Assess practical effect sizes, stability, statistical uncertainty and consistency.

### Robustness testing

Evaluate justified alternatives for:

- Training-window selection.
- City inclusion.
- Forecast horizon.
- Major regulatory regimes.
- Data-proxy definitions.
- Outlier treatment.
- Historical publication uncertainty.
- Model flexibility.

Label all post-hoc experiments exploratory.

**Deliverables:** Reproducible rolling-origin predictions, city/horizon/target comparisons, uncertainty estimates, H1/H2 verdicts, robustness results and independently checked evaluation metrics.

**Gate:** No final research conclusions until significant calculations and empirical claims have been verified.

# 8. Stage 9 — Interpretation, Scenarios and Research Paper

**Objective:** Convert verified empirical results into a scientifically defensible paper.

### Interpretation

Explain:

- Which methods performed best and under what conditions.
- Whether simple baselines outperformed complex models.
- Whether city-specific or shared models were more successful.
- How performance changed across forecast horizons and target definitions.
- Whether H1 and H2 received support.
- Which findings were sensitive, contradictory or inconclusive.
- How data limitations restrict generalization.

Never infer causality from predictor importance alone.

### Scenarios

Only perform scenario analysis if the models and available evidence justify it.

Clearly label conditional predictive projections and distinguish them from causal policy-impact estimates.

Do not manufacture scenarios or impacts merely to make the project appear more comprehensive.

### Research manuscript

Prepare:

1. Abstract.
2. Introduction and research objectives.
3. Literature review.
4. Indian CNG pricing context.
5. Data, provenance and limitations.
6. Research methodology and experimental design.
7. Forecasting results.
8. Diagnostics, uncertainty and robustness.
9. Discussion and alternative explanations.
10. Conclusions and future research.
11. References and reproducibility appendix.

The paper must report negative findings, failed approaches and methodological limitations where material.

Every quantitative statement, chart and table must be traceable to executable calculations and accepted data.

# 9. NEW — Mandatory Experiment Registry and Research Transparency

Maintain an auditable experiment register throughout Stages 4–9.

For every consequential experiment, record:

- Unique experiment ID and timestamp.
- Data snapshot and checksums.
- Research question and predeclared hypothesis.
- Model or method.
- Variables and transformations.
- Training, validation and test windows.
- Hyperparameters and random seeds.
- Software/environment versions.
- Metrics, uncertainty estimates and diagnostics.
- Failures, warnings and limitations.
- Decision: accepted, rejected, revised or inconclusive.
- Whether the experiment was confirmatory or exploratory.
- Deviations from the original experimental plan.
- Reason for any subsequent modification.

Do not erase failed experiments or selectively present favorable outcomes.

Protect the untouched final test period. If it becomes part of iterative model selection, explicitly acknowledge that it is no longer an independent final test and establish a defensible new evaluation strategy if possible.

Maintain a complete link between manuscript claims, experiment IDs, outputs and underlying data.

# 10. NEW — Independent Scientific Audit

At the completion of each stage, conduct a verification pass independent of the original analysis wherever practicable.

### Audit requirements

**Data audit**
- Verify primary sources and provenance.
- Inspect time boundaries and missingness.
- Test source consistency.

**Calculation audit**
- Independently recompute important aggregates and performance metrics.
- Test boundary cases and formulas.
- Reconcile discrepancies.

**Methodology audit**
- Evaluate statistical assumptions.
- Inspect leakage risks.
- Check whether the model design suits the sample.

**Inference audit**
- Assess uncertainty, statistical power and multiple testing.
- Challenge claims of improvement or significance.

**Reporting audit**
- Match every result in the paper to actual output.
- Verify citations and definitions.
- Confirm negative and inconclusive outcomes are represented faithfully.

A second execution of the same faulty calculation does not count as independent verification.

If independent verification fails, the affected claim must not appear as established fact.

# 11. Mandatory Failure, Recovery and Rejection Protocol

Whenever data, assumptions, calculations or empirical findings fail:

1. **Detect:** Identify the specific failure or contradiction.
2. **Verify:** Independently inspect sources, mathematics, methodology and code.
3. **Classify:** Decide whether the problem concerns data authenticity, implementation, assumptions, statistical power, uncertainty or genuine lack of predictive value.
4. **Preserve:** Retain the original dataset, implementation and failed result.
5. **Repair or reject:** Correct independently demonstrated errors. Reject inappropriate methods or unsupported hypotheses.
6. **Rerun:** Repeat all affected downstream analyses using versioned, justified corrections.
7. **Document:** Record what changed and whether the scientific conclusion changed.
8. **Escalate:** If essential evidence is insufficient, stop and request researcher review.

Never overwrite original observations or quietly modify definitions to obtain expected results.

Do not endlessly search for a specification that supports the desired hypothesis.

**If the original research question is unsupported by available evidence, recommend a transparent revision—not an artificial positive result.**

# FINAL DIRECTIVE

**We do not need our hypotheses to be correct. We need our research to be correct.**

GAM can lose.

Numerical predictors can fail.

News and corporate sentiment can provide no improvement.

Complex models can underperform naïve forecasts.

Statistical relationships can be insignificant.

A dataset can be unsuitable despite having many rows.

These are all legitimate research outcomes.

Do not fabricate, manipulate, conceal or selectively report evidence.

Aim for the highest achievable standard of authenticity, transparency, independent verification and reproducibility. Never equate successful execution with scientific validity or claim certainty beyond the evidence.

**Remain at Stage 3 until the accepted-data checkpoint is approved. Then conduct Stages 4–9 sequentially, with critical evaluation and researcher approval at every stage.**

**Scientific integrity takes precedence over model accuracy, complexity, favorable hypotheses and publication expectations.**
