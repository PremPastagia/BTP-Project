## 4. Stage 5 — Freeze the forecasting design and engineer features

**Purpose:** Specify a fair experiment before inspecting final-test performance.

1. Define forecast origins, target months, and the exact meaning of each horizon.
2. Prespecify how the two targets will be evaluated and identify the primary comparison.
3. Choose chronological development and final-test periods based on actual usable coverage.
4. Use rolling-origin or expanding-window validation.
5. Ensure training labels were observable by each forecast origin, including for overlapping multi-step targets.
6. Decide whether city-specific, pooled, or both model types are justified.
7. Define feasible evaluation rules for short-history cities.
8. Engineer parsimonious features such as target lags, past price changes, time since the last known revision, trailing summaries, and available external variables.
9. Align each predictor to its actual or conservatively assumed availability.
10. Identify already-lagged fields to prevent accidental double-lagging.
11. Do not use realized future covariates. Use origin-available values, separately forecast covariates, or explicitly labeled scenarios.
12. Audit text timestamps and city/operator relevance. Exclude retrospective summaries and target-revealing material unavailable at the origin.
13. Freeze model candidates, tuning budgets, metrics, sample rules, and comparison procedures.

Use MAE, RMSE, and MASE where defined. Specify MASE denominators and handling of constant series. Treat percentage metrics cautiously. Prespecify uncertainty methods and interval evaluation if probabilistic forecasts are included.

**Deliverables:** experiment specification, split manifest, feature dictionary, availability rules, and leakage checks.

**Completion gate:** Every model comparison can be reproduced using information available at the same historical origins.

## 5. Stage 6 — Establish forecasting baselines

**Purpose:** Measure how much value modeling adds over simple forecasts.

Evaluate:

- Last observed price.
- Seasonal naive, where sufficient history and plausible seasonality exist.
- Drift.
- Appropriate statistical models, such as ETS, ARIMA, or dynamic regression.
- A simple regularized regression where appropriate.

CNG prices can remain unchanged for long periods. Treat persistence as a serious benchmark.

Use identical eligible origins, horizons, and target definitions for paired comparisons. Report city-level and horizon-level performance, along with clearly defined aggregate summaries.

For dynamic regression, obey the future-covariate rules established in Stage 5.

**Deliverables:** executable baseline pipeline, saved predictions, run records, metrics, diagnostics, and baseline comparison tables.

**Completion gate:** Baseline results trace to actual runs and provide a fair reference for advanced models.
